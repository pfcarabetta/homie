Every item in your report carries a severity flag. The colors are deliberate — red is "stop and think," orange is "should fix," grey is "keep an eye on it." Triage your time by color before anything else.

## The five levels

| Color | Label | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Red | **Safety hazard** | Active danger to people in the home — gas leak, exposed wiring, structural defect. Address immediately. |
| 🔴 Red | **Urgent** | Not a hazard yet, but will cause serious damage if ignored — active leak, electrical issue affecting whole circuit, roof flashing failure. Fix in the next 1–4 weeks. |
| 🟠 Orange | **Recommended** | Real defects that need attention but aren't time-critical — worn seal, scratched countertop, minor pest activity. Fix when convenient, factor into negotiations. |
| ⚪ Grey | **Monitor** | Cosmetic or early-stage issues. Watch them so they don't become Recommended later. Don't usually warrant negotiation leverage. |
| ⚪ Grey | **Informational** | Notes from the inspector for context — "this is a 2018 Rheem water heater," "kitchen was remodeled in 2020." Not defects. |

## How to use the colors

**If you're a buyer in the contingency window**:
- Build your repair request from **Safety hazards + Urgent + Recommended**. Be picky on Recommended — pick the ones that cost real money or affect livability.
- Skip Monitor items in the request. Mention them in conversation if you want, but they shouldn't be in the formal ask. Asking for cosmetic credits weakens your stronger requests.

**If you're a seller pre-listing**:
- Fix every **Safety hazard** before anything goes on MLS — your inspector flagged it for a reason and the buyer's inspector will too.
- Fix **Urgent** items unless you can disclose them clearly with documentation.
- **Recommended** items are your call: fix the cheap ones (under $200), disclose the expensive ones, see what the market does.

## How severity gets assigned

The AI reads your inspector's language ("this is a safety hazard," "we recommend addressing this," "for monitoring") and maps it to our five-level scale. We err on the side of bumping things UP a level when there's ambiguity — better to over-flag than under-flag.

If something feels miscategorized, you can:
- Open the AI Deep Dive on that item and ask "should this really be Recommended?"
- The Deep Dive will tell you what the inspector actually said and whether the severity feels right

## Filtering by severity

In the **Items** and **Negotiations** tabs, you can filter to one severity level (or a combination). Most buyers start by filtering to "Safety + Urgent + Recommended" and reviewing those before anything else.
